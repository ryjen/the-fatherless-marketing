The lie beneath the Republic

He needed his victims to be guilty

An elite slaveholder hides a truth from two enslaved people and arranges the conditions for a private scandal only he understands. Neither knows what he knows. Their child is supposed to become evidence for slavery. Instead, Yasael survives.

Years later, the Republic must decide what Yasael is—property, threat, symbol, or person—while the secret of his birth threatens the man who made it and the social order that needs his argument to remain plausible.

World

Senate politics, elite households, slavery, public spectacle, and persecuted communities coexist inside a Republic confident that order itself proves virtue.

Conflict

The man who manufactured the scandal can survive exposure only if the Republic keeps asking who owns Yasael instead of what was done to create him.

Question

What makes authority legitimate when institutions can legalize cruelty—and what remains when a person refuses to answer coercion with coercion?
