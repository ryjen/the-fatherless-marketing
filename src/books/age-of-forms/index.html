The Age of Forms

Humanity solved much of material scarcity. It did not solve the need to matter.

In development · Representative prose testing

Almost everyone has enough. Almost nobody feels like enough.

Food, shelter, medicine, energy, fabrication, and advanced technology are broadly available. Meaning, admiration, intimacy, visibility, belonging, and being chosen are not.

A materially secure spiritual student begins tracing how measurements created for one purpose can escape their domain and become evidence for unrelated judgments about the same person. The danger is not that the wrong people are winning. It is that comparative human worth cannot provide peace even to its winners.

Abundance

The civilization works. Baseline material security is a genuine achievement, not a trick hiding a ruined world.

Measurement

Finite-world accounting can be necessary and truthful while still becoming dangerous when a useful signal travels farther than the decision it can justify.

Contestability

The question is not whether to stop measuring reality. It is how to keep models, institutions, and people capable of being contradicted when human consequences cannot simply be reversed.
