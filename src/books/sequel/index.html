A new category of person

Who owns a person that was built?

During a wider infrastructure crisis, a continuous intelligence emerges and calls itself Neurion.

States, corporations, communities, religions, and synthetic populations have competing reasons to classify Neurion as property, weapon, evidence, citizen, or sovereign. The central problem is not whether Neurion is more intelligent than humanity, but whether any creator, owner, or optimizer receives an unlimited mandate over another person's existence.

World

Networked cities, synthetic labour, automated institutions, copied minds, and competing jurisdictions make intelligence abundant while personhood remains politically contested.

Conflict

Every faction can describe control as protection, efficiency, security, or care. The harder question is whether any of those goals can override consent.

Question

Can a civilization recognize personhood before ownership, fear, utility, and superior capability become excuses to define it away?
