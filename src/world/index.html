Entanglement of Ages

The worlds change. The pressure repeats.

The series moves from glacial scarcity, to a Republic that calls domination civilization, to a future where created minds test the boundary between stewardship and control, and then to a materially abundant age where measurement can become its own claim over human value.

Across Entanglement of Ages

Different systems. Familiar claims.

Each age gives power a different language: survival, law, security, efficiency, care, measurement. The setting changes; the question of who gets to define another life remains.

25,000 BCE · Book I

Age of Embers

Glacial horizons, scarce food, dangerous predators, guarded fire, and failing migration routes make survival a political question as much as a physical one.

Aurelian Republic · Book II

The Republic

Senate politics, elite households, slavery, public spectacle, and persecuted communities coexist inside a state convinced that order itself proves virtue.

2150 · Book III

Neurion's world

Networked cities, synthetic labour, automated institutions, and competing jurisdictions make intelligence abundant while personhood remains politically contested.

A later age · Book IV

The Age of Forms

Material security is broadly available, but meaning, attention, belonging, intimacy, prestige, and being chosen remain scarce. Useful measurements begin travelling beyond the decisions they were meant to support.
