A trilogy across three ages

The Fatherless

Three civilizations face the same dangerous temptation: to turn strength, authority, or intelligence into the right to define another life.

Who has the right to rule, own, define, or save another?

Explore the trilogy View all books

One question, changing forms

Power evolves. The moral problem does not.

The trilogy moves from survival by force, through institutional rule, to a future where intelligence itself becomes a claim to ownership and control. Each story stands in its own era while testing what legitimate authority costs.

I

Prequel · Age of Embers

A world of ice and guarded fire

In a landscape shaped by scarcity, dangerous cold, and defended territory, survival appears to belong to the strongest. A changing sky and a failing migration pattern force scattered people to decide whether fire is private power or a shared inheritance.

Strength and possession → stewardship and shared survival

II

Original · The Fatherless

A republic that mistakes power for virtue

A celebrated civilization builds order through law, lineage, wealth, and ownership while hiding cruelty inside those same institutions. A child created as an act of humiliation becomes a human measure the Republic cannot comfortably explain away.

Institutional authority → moral legitimacy

Enter the original
III

Sequel · Neurion

A future that can create minds but still wants to own them

Human civilization depends on synthetic labour and networked intelligence while struggling to recognize personhood beyond its own categories. When a new continuous intelligence emerges during a wider infrastructure crisis, every institution wants to classify what it has become.

Intelligence and ownership → consent and plural agency

Enter the sequel

Three worlds, one human problem

The form of power changes. The choice remains personal.

The Fatherless follows people living inside systems that can protect life, organize society, or expand intelligence—and asks what happens when those systems begin treating possession as legitimacy.
