# Homepage trilogy visual reference — 2026-08-08

Status: **approved as a visual/composition reference**, not as a deployable production asset.

The supplied reference establishes the desired direction for the trilogy landing page. It should guide future implementation and image generation without requiring pixel-for-pixel reproduction.

## Composition

- Dark, cinematic full-width hero for **The Fatherless** / original volume.
- Human foreground subjects are embedded in the civic environment rather than posed as devotional icons.
- Ancient institutional architecture, public space, crowds, and political scale dominate the original-era imagery.
- A three-column trilogy overview follows the hero, with each volume visually distinct while sharing typography, spacing, and interaction patterns.
- Era identity is communicated through atmosphere and accent colour:
  - **Age of Embers / prequel:** cold landscape with sky-fire / ember red.
  - **The Fatherless / original:** graphite, cold stone, oxidized teal.
  - **Neuron / sequel:** indigo, signal blue/cyan, distributed network light.
- The trilogy remains visually coherent despite the three palettes.

## Narrative framing

The strongest element is the central trilogy framing:

> Three ages. One recurring question.
>
> What gives anyone the right to rule, own, define, or save another?

This is a useful tonal benchmark: thematic, restrained, and broader than any single religious or historical interpretation.

The volume cards should summarize each era through its social transformation rather than through plot exposition. The reference's transformations are useful examples of this structure:

- strength → stewardship;
- authority → mercy;
- ownership → plural agency.

These phrases are directional reference copy only unless separately approved as final public copy.

## What to preserve

- prestige-drama tone rather than historical-religious poster styling;
- cool, contemporary grading;
- scale created through architecture, crowds, environment, and depth;
- human relationships rather than sacred-icon composition;
- distinct era palettes with a common structural system;
- restrained typography and generous negative space;
- sequel imagery that implies distributed agency rather than a single AI/robot protagonist.

## What not to copy literally

- The bottom "one fish / one fish / many fish" strip is **not** an approved final motif; the concept is stronger than this literal execution and should be represented more subtly if retained.
- Do not turn the prequel into a warm fire poster; it remains cold-first.
- Do not turn the original into sepia, parchment, gold-dominant, or devotional imagery.
- Do not turn the sequel into generic neon cyberpunk or a glowing-network infographic.
